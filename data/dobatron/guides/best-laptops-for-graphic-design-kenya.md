Short answer: for CAD, 3D and video editing, get dedicated graphics. The HP ZBook Firefly 14 G8 (32GB, 4GB graphics) is the strongest laptop we list, and the EliteBook 745 G6 with 2GB graphics is a lower-cost start. For design on macOS, choose the 2018 MacBook Pro.

Design software leans on two things: memory, for large files and many layers, and the graphics chip, for 3D, CAD, effects and video timelines. Office laptops share memory with a built-in graphics chip; workstations add a dedicated graphics card with its own memory.

## What matters

**Dedicated graphics for 3D, CAD and video.** Programs like AutoCAD, SketchUp, Blender and video editors run far better with a dedicated card. Photo editing and 2D design are fine on built-in graphics if you have enough RAM.

**16GB of RAM or more.** Large Photoshop files, video timelines and 3D scenes fill memory quickly. 32GB is comfortable for heavy video and 3D work.

**512GB of storage.** Design files and video footage are big. Plan on an external drive for archives either way.

**Colour.** Most business laptop screens are not made for colour-critical print work. If colour accuracy matters, pair the laptop with a calibrated external monitor.

**A pen for sketching.** 2-in-1 laptops with a stylus are handy for sketches and markup, though not a replacement for a drawing tablet.

## FAQ

### Do I need dedicated graphics for Photoshop?

Not always. Photo editing and graphic design run on built-in graphics with 16GB of RAM. 3D, CAD and video editing benefit from a dedicated graphics card.

### Is a workstation worth it over an ordinary business laptop?

Only if your software uses the graphics card heavily. For design plus everyday office work, a 16GB business laptop costs less. See the [ZBook page](/laptops/hp-zbook) for current workstation prices.

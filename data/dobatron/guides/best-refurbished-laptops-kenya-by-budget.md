Refurbished business laptops spent their first years in offices, then were inspected and restored. They were built for daily office use, so a good one outlasts a new budget laptop at the same price. ## First, decode the model name

**HP.** EliteBook is HP's business line. The 830 is 13.3-inch and the 840 is 14-inch. A model ending in 5 (745, 845) is the AMD Ryzen version. ProBook is HP's mid-range line, and ZBook is the workstation line with dedicated graphics. The "G" number is the generation: G5 and G6 have 8th Gen Intel chips, G7 has 10th Gen and G8 has 11th Gen.

**Lenovo.** Everything here is a ThinkPad. X models are small and light, T models are the mainstream 14-inch line, and X1 is the premium flagship. "Yoga" in the name means it flips over into a tablet.

**Dell.** Latitude is Dell's business line: 5000 series for mainstream, 7000 series for premium. The second digit is roughly the screen size, so the 5300 is 13-inch, the 5400 is 14-inch and the 7280 is 12-inch.

## Two rules for buying in 2026

**Get 8th Gen Intel or newer if you'll run Windows.** Windows 10 stopped getting free security updates in October 2025, and Windows 11 officially needs an 8th Gen Intel chip or newer. Older laptops still work, but they stay on an unsupported Windows. Every listing on Dobatron has a "Windows 11" tag so you don't have to work it out.

**8GB of RAM is the minimum, 16GB if you multitask.** 8GB handles Office, browsing and Zoom. Go for 16GB if you keep dozens of tabs open, edit photos, or run several apps at once.

## What to check before you pay

Whoever you buy from, check these on the actual unit:

- **Battery health.** On Windows, open Command Prompt, run `powercfg /batteryreport` and open the file it creates. Compare "Full charge capacity" with "Design capacity". Above 80% is good.
- **No company lock.** Restart and open the BIOS setup: it shouldn't ask for a password. In Windows Settings, you shouldn't see "Some settings are managed by your organization".
- **Keyboard and trackpad.** Open Notepad and press every key.
- **Screen.** View a plain white image and a plain black one to spot dead pixels or pressure marks.
- **Ports and charger.** Test every USB port, the charging port and HDMI if you need it. Make sure the charger is the right wattage.
- **Wi-Fi, webcam, mic and speakers.** One quick video call tests all four.
